## 3. ⚠️ PUNITIVE DAMAGES ARE PLEADED — this was not mentioned anywhere else in the file

**Counts 3, 6 and 9 each allege, in identical language:**

> "Defendants' **fraud, oppression and/or malice** was a substantial factor in causing
> Plaintiff's harm and as such Plaintiff requests this court award Plaintiff… **punitive damages
> against Defendants pursuant to California Civil Code 3294.**"

**Neither our mediation brief nor the § 998 offer nor the July 2026 cover letter mentions
punitive damages at all.** Counsel should be aware this is in the operative pleading.

**Why counsel should evaluate this early — three concerns, stated as questions, not conclusions:**

1. **Each punitive count is pleaded on breach of the implied covenant**, which in California
   generally sounds in **contract**. Counsel should evaluate whether § 3294 punitive damages are
   available on these counts absent an independently pleaded tort. **No tort cause of action is
   pleaded anywhere in the Complaint** — there is no fraud count, no conversion count, no breach
   of fiduciary duty count.
2. **The "fraud, oppression and/or malice" allegation is conclusory.** It recites the statutory
   elements without supporting facts, and fraud ordinarily requires particularity.
3. **Defendants are sued solely in their representative capacity** as successor co-trustees.
   Counsel should consider against whom punitive damages would run and from what assets they
   would be satisfied.

**Practical exposure:** these three counts are a natural target for a **motion to strike** or
demurrer. Because defendants have **not yet responded** under an open-ended extension, that
motion has not been filed — **but it is available to them at any time, including in the run-up
to the CMC.** Counsel may wish to decide proactively whether to amend rather than defend them.
**This is a legal question this workspace cannot answer** — the research environment could not
verify authority (see `../02-legal-research/00-READ-FIRST-research-reliability.md`).

## 4. ⚠️ The prayer asks the court to order defendants to "EMPLOY" two non-parties

Every specific-performance count asks for **"an order from the court to employ Correia-Xavier"**
or **"to employ MOSS ADAMS jointly."** The declaratory counts ask for a declaration that the
parties **"are required to jointly use"** those firms, with **"discounts applied as determined in
his/their professional opinion."**

This is the exact structure research memo **R2** flagged against **Civ. Code § 3390(b)**
(no specific enforcement of "an obligation to employ another in personal service") and
**§ 3390(d)** (no order compelling a party "to procure the act or consent of… any other third
person"). **Neither Correia-Xavier nor Moss Adams is a party.** Defendants' brief further notes
Moss Adams is **"now Baker Tilly."**

R2 suggests an alternative framing — judicial valuation, appointment under **CCP § 1281.6**
(court "shall appoint" where the agreed method fails), or **Evid. Code § 730**.

> **⚠️ R2's § 3390 analysis is UNVERIFIED.** The statutory text was read verbatim; the case-law
> gloss was not. **Counsel must confirm in Westlaw/CoCounsel before acting.** But the *fit*
> between the statute's words and this prayer is close enough that it should be checked before
> the CMC, and before any amendment deadline.

**Note the same structure carries into the § 998 offer**, which offers judgment requiring
defendants to "employ Stanley Xavier" and "employ MOSS ADAMS." If the pleaded relief is
problematic, the § 998 offer inherits the problem. See `adverse_analysis.md` A-3.

### 5.1 — The prior Xavier appraisal used a **20%–30%** discount range

> **¶17.** "For valuing the Real Property at that time, the Hazel and Steve agreed to use real

**⚠️ Our three stated discount positions do not match each other:**

| Source | Minority discount |
|---|---|
| Complaint ¶17 (prior Xavier appraisal) | **20%–30%**, varying by property |
| March 3, 2025 offer ($3,950,565.28) | **30%** |

Counsel should reconcile these before the CMC. The July 2026 letter's characterization of 25% as
"consistent with" the prior Xavier work is defensible against a **20–30% range**, but it is not
the 30% we demanded in March 2025, and the movement is documented in our own correspondence.

### 5.2 — **Moss Adams found NO errors in the Xavier appraisal** (Complaint Exhibit Q)

> **¶45.** "Moss Adams also provided a review letter as it pertains to the two Real Property
> appraisals. In their summary, Moss Adams found that **both appraisals were valid** and that the
> two appraisers used different approaches. Notably, **Moss Adams specifically found that there
> were no errors or an improper appraisal performed by Correia Xavier.**"

**This is the strongest fact in the Complaint and it goes directly to the heart of defendants'
case.** Defendants' whole justification for rejecting the Xavier appraisal is that it "contained
errors" — they attach a schedule of them as their Exhibit D. **A neutral they themselves
retained reportedly found otherwise.**

**Action: obtain Complaint Exhibit Q (the Moss Adams review letter) and read it directly.** It is
not in this workspace. If it says what ¶45 says it says, it is a centerpiece document.
See `open_items.md` OI-20.

### 5.3 — The Xavier appraisal was commissioned for **IRS Form 706**, not for the buyout

> **¶73, ¶96.** "Defendants then unilaterally had Correia-Xavier perform a real estate appraisal
> for purposes of filing for a **IRS 706 filing** but refused to perform a Real Property appraisal
> to include the parameters as defined in the Settlement Agreement…"

**Counsel should treat this as double-edged.** It explains why the appraisal came back at 100%
ownership with no discounts — a 706 valuation would not apply buyout discounts. **But it also
undercuts our own position** that the Xavier appraisal is the binding buyout appraisal under
TIC § 11: on our own pleading, it was commissioned for a *different purpose*. See
`adverse_analysis.md` A-15.
