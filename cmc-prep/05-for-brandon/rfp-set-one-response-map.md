## 1. "They're still looking for stolen funds." Is that what this is? Both ways.

**Yes, in substance, the demands are built around a diversion theory.** They fall into three
groups:

| Group | Demands | The question behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Income that might not reach the books | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5; 6 (cash rent) | Were sales or rent taken in cash or deposited somewhere other than Citizens? |
| Partnership money spent on non-dairy things | 11–18 | Construction, insurance, life insurance, hardware, fuel, vehicle repair, electrical: where, and for whose property? |
| Capital-account entries that move value between partners | 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 | Were Hazel's draws overstated, Steve's contributions overstated, the Bypass transfer proper, the balloon a partnership debt? |

The demands are specific: check numbers, invoice-level detail, "location of services rendered,"
"confirmation that the expense was a DAIRY expense." So their CPA has picked entries that look
personal *on their face*. That is the groundwork for the answer and cross-complaint Cunningham
said is coming (kit §2.6).

**But the demands do not, by themselves, show they believe Steve stole money.** Counsel should
expect them to give, and a court to hear, these other reasons, all ordinary:
- **Valuation.** When a business interest is valued, personal expenses run through the business
  are usually added back to income ("normalization"). Higher income means higher value.
  Demands 11–18 feed that adjustment whether or not anyone calls it theft.
- **Capital accounts.** Every offer since 3/3/2025 nets a **$262,592 excess-distribution
  offset** (OI-60). Demands 6–8 test the numbers behind it, as we would.
- **Post-death profits.** If they claim a share of post-death profits (post-death memo), every
  post-death expense lowers that share. Built Wright and the legal fees bear on it.
- **Their role as trustees.** Trustees arguably have to investigate the trust's main asset.
  That is a legal point, UNVERIFIED, for counsel.
- **Our own record invited several of these:**
  - the 5/1/2026 draft saying rent cash "bypasses the formal ledger" (A-68);
  - the 3/6/2026 answer admitting the dairy insures Steve's two rental houses, Jace's house and
    personal vehicles, and pays "some personal fuel" (kit §2.4);
  - "no cash hay sales" against $121,148 of 2024 crop sales (kit item 1).

**Bottom line for Jace:** treat this as an audit, not an accusation. The best answer to a
"funneling" story is clean, complete, *third-party* records. That means vendor statements,
policy schedules, bank images and a permit. Where the dairy did pay something personal, our
side should be the one to identify it and say how it was charged. Late or partial production
feeds the story, and Ormonde has already warned that withheld documents can be barred at trial.
