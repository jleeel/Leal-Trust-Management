## Issue

1. What does Probate Code § 16060 *et seq.* require of a trustee by way of keeping beneficiaries informed and rendering accounts (§§ 16060, 16061, 16062, 16063, 16064)?
2. Who is entitled to an account, and what must a compliant account contain?
3. What does Probate Code § 17200 permit — who has standing to petition to compel an account, and what relief may the probate court grant?
4. What limitations periods and equitable time bars apply to accounting and breach-of-trust claims (§§ 16460, 16461; laches)?
5. What remedies exist for **omitted estate assets** — property that should have been, but was not, inventoried into or included in a trust estate (§ 850 *et seq.*; § 859 double damages)?
6. What are the standards for **surcharging** a trustee (§§ 16400, 16401, 16402, 16403, 16420, 16440, 16441)? What must be proven, who bears the burden, is bad faith required, and how are damages measured?
7. Does a trustee's failure to account shift any burden to the trustee, and is there authority construing gaps and ambiguities in trust records against the fiduciary who had the duty to keep them?
8. What is a trustee's duty to account for a **partnership interest** held by the trust, and how do partnership-level books interact with trust-level accounting?

**Cross-cutting:** Because our client is both the plaintiff-beneficiary *and* the managing partner who kept the partnership books — and possibly a fiduciary of one or more of the related trusts — this memo analyzes the accounting-duty authority **in both directions**, including squarely against our own side.

## Short Answer

1. **The duty is layered, and only the outer layer is waivable.** § 16060 imposes a general, affirmative duty to keep beneficiaries reasonably informed. § 16061 adds a duty to furnish information on reasonable request. § 16062(a) requires an account at least annually, at termination, and on a change of trustee. § 16063 dictates minimum contents. § 16064 lists the exceptions — trust-instrument waiver, beneficiary waiver, and the revocable-trust period under § 15800 — but even a waiver yields where the court finds it "reasonably likely that a material breach of the trust has occurred."

2. **Entitlement runs to current income/principal beneficiaries under § 16062(a); remainder beneficiaries get information and can compel through § 17200.** A compliant account must contain the five categories in § 16063(a), including a notice advising the recipient of the right to seek court review and — critically for limitations purposes — a notice of the three-year bar. A *court-filed* account additionally must satisfy §§ 1060–1064.

3. **§ 17200 is the vehicle, and the court's remedial power under § 17206 is broad.** § 17200(b)(5) reaches settling accounts and passing on trustee acts; § 17200(b)(7) reaches compelling information and accounts, subject to a 60-day-after-written-request predicate (and, for accounts, a no-account-within-six-months predicate).

4. **Three years is the governing period under § 16460, but the trigger differs.** If a written account or report *adequately disclosed* the claim, three years runs from receipt. If it did not, or none was ever received, three years runs from actual or constructive discovery. Exculpatory clauses under § 16461 cannot reach intentional, grossly negligent, bad-faith, or recklessly indifferent breaches, or profits derived from a breach.

5. **Omitted assets are addressed by a § 850 petition, with § 856 relief and § 859 double damages.** § 850(a)(3) expressly covers both directions — trustee holding property claimed by another, and trustee claiming property held by another. *Heggstad* supplies the substantive route for property never formally re-titled. **§ 859 requires bad faith**; *Levin v. Winston-Levin* holds that even a finding of undue influence does not by itself supply it.

6. **Surcharge requires a breach plus causation plus loss — not bad faith.** § 16400 makes any duty violation a breach; § 16440(a) supplies three alternative measures (loss with interest, trustee's profit with interest, lost profit); § 16441 fixes interest at the greater of the legal judgment rate or interest actually received. Bad faith is *not* an element — but its **absence** is what unlocks the § 16440(b) and § 16441(b) equitable excuse, and its **presence** is what defeats § 16461 exculpation and unlocks § 859. *Uzyel v. Kadisha* confirms lost-profit/appreciation damages and disgorgement without tracing.

7. **Yes — this is the strongest doctrinal lever in the memo, and it cuts both ways.** *Purdy v. Johnson* and *Estate of McCabe* place the burden on the accounting fiduciary to prove every item by satisfactory evidence, and resolve every doubt arising from the fiduciary's own failure to keep proper records **against** the fiduciary. This is a rule about *whoever kept the books.*

8. **A partnership interest does not shrink the trust-level duty; it is simply one asset that must be reported.** Corporations Code § 16403 gives partners *and former partners* a right of access to partnership books, which means a trustee holding a partnership interest generally cannot plead inability to obtain the underlying records. Probate Code § 1061 treats a trade or business as a net-reported item with a supporting schedule. **No California case squarely addressing trust-level accounting for a partnership interest was located.**

**Bidirectional caution:** The § 16060-series and *Purdy*/*McCabe* record-keeping authority is not plaintiff-friendly by nature. It is **book-keeper-hostile**. Our client kept the partnership books. To the extent he also served in a fiduciary capacity as to the Bypass Trust or the partnership interest, the same authority we would deploy is available in mirror image.

### C. Limitations, exculpation, consent, and release

**Cal. Prob. Code § 16460** — **VERIFIED-BY-SEARCH**
- If a beneficiary **has received** an interim or final account in writing, or other written report, **that adequately discloses the existence of a claim** against the trustee for breach of trust, the claim is barred as to that beneficiary unless a proceeding to assert it is commenced **within three years after receipt** of the account or report.
- *"An account or report adequately discloses existence of a claim if it provides sufficient information so that the beneficiary knows of the claim or reasonably should have inquired into the existence of the claim."*
- If the account or report **does not adequately disclose** the claim, **or if the beneficiary received no written account or report at all**, the claim is barred unless a proceeding is commenced **within three years after the beneficiary discovered, or reasonably should have discovered, the subject of the claim.**

Source: `https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-prob/division-9/part-4/chapter-4/article-4/section-16460/`
**Subdivision lettering not confirmed — the retrieval returned the substance without reliable (a)/(b) designations.**

**Estate of Heggstad (1993) 16 Cal.App.4th 943** — **PARAPHRASE-ONLY**

### E. Surcharge — breach, measure of liability, burden

**Cal. Prob. Code § 16400** — **VERIFIED-BY-SEARCH**
Text returned: *"A violation by the trustee of any duty that the trustee owes the beneficiary is a breach of trust."* History returned as: Enacted by Stats. 1990, Ch. 79.
Source: `https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-prob/division-9/part-4/chapter-4/article-1/section-16400/`

**Cal. Prob. Code § 16401 (agents)** — **PARAPHRASE-ONLY**
*"Except as provided in subdivision (b), the trustee is not liable to the beneficiary for the acts or omissions of an agent."* The trustee **is** liable for an agent's act or omission that would be a breach if committed by the trustee where the trustee: directs the act; delegates authority the trustee had a duty not to delegate; fails to use reasonable prudence in selecting or retaining the agent; **fails to periodically review the agent's overall performance and compliance**; conceals the agent's act; or neglects to take reasonable steps to compel the agent to redress the wrong.

**Cal. Prob. Code § 16402 (cotrustees)** — **PARAPHRASE-ONLY**
A trustee is liable to the beneficiary for a breach committed by a **cotrustee** where the trustee: participates in the cotrustee's breach; improperly delegates administration to the cotrustee; **approves, knowingly acquiesces in, or conceals** the cotrustee's breach; negligently enables the cotrustee to commit the breach; or **neglects to take reasonable steps to compel the cotrustee to redress** the breach.
Source: `https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-prob/division-9/part-4/chapter-4/article-1/section-16402/`

**Cal. Prob. Code § 16403 (successor trustees)** — **PARAPHRASE-ONLY**

Source: `https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-prob/division-9/part-4/chapter-4/article-1/section-16403/`

### F. Failure to account; construing gaps against the record-keeping fiduciary

This is the doctrinal core of Issue 7, and the two cases below are old, durable, and frequently cited.

**Purdy v. Johnson (1917) 174 Cal. 521** — **VERIFIED-BY-SEARCH** (language returned as apparently verbatim)
Reported language: *"whatever doubts arise from a trustee's failure to keep proper records or inability to establish the items of their accounts must be resolved against them"*; this result *"follows from the failure and neglect of the trustees to perform their duty of keeping full and accurate accounts of their transactions."* Further: *"it is the duty of the trustees to support every item of their account, and wherever they fail to support the correctness of a charge or credit by satisfactory evidence, the item must be disallowed."*
Sources: `https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/5914a8fdadd7b04934707f6b`; also surfaced in *Estate of Bissinger*, `https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2d/60/756.html`

**Estate of McCabe (1950) 98 Cal.App.2d 503** — **VERIFIED-BY-SEARCH** (language returned as apparently verbatim)

### G. Partnership interest held by a trust; partnership books vs. trust accounting

**No California case addressing trust-level accounting for a partnership interest was located. This is a genuine gap in this memo. — UNVERIFIED.** The analysis below is assembled from statutes, each individually sourced.

**Cal. Corp. Code § 16403 (partnership books and records)** — **VERIFIED-BY-SEARCH**
- *"A partnership shall keep its books and records, if any, in writing or in any other form capable of being converted into clearly legible tangible form, at its chief executive office."*
- The partnership *"shall provide partners and their agents and attorneys access to its books and records, and shall provide **former partners** and their agents and attorneys access to books and records pertaining to the period during which they were partners."* The right of access includes the opportunity to **inspect and copy** during ordinary business hours; a reasonable copying charge may be imposed.
- *"Each partner and the partnership shall furnish to a partner … any information concerning the partnership's business and affairs reasonably required for the proper exercise of the partner's rights and duties under the partnership agreement or this chapter, and on demand, any other information concerning the partnership's business and affairs, except to the extent the demand or the information demanded is unreasonable or otherwise improper under the circumstances."*

Source: `https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-corp/title-2/chapter-5/article-4/section-16403/`
**Subdivision lettering not confirmed.**

> **This is the pivot for Issue 8.** A trustee holding a partnership interest is (or holds the interest of) a partner, and § 16403 gives partners and former partners a statutory right of access to the books. That right substantially undercuts a trustee's defense that it "could not obtain" partnership-level information. It equally means that **the partner who *holds* the books owes production** — including to a trustee-partner and, derivatively, to that trust's beneficiaries.

**Cal. Corp. Code § 16404 (partner fiduciary duties)** — **VERIFIED-BY-SEARCH**
- The fiduciary duties a partner owes to the partnership and the other partners are the **duty of loyalty** and the **duty of care** set forth in subdivisions (b) and (c).
- **Duty of loyalty** includes *"[t]o account to the partnership and hold as trustee for it any property, profit, or benefit derived by the partner in the conduct and winding up of the partnership business or derived from a use by the partner of partnership property or information, including the appropriation of a partnership opportunity"*; refraining from dealing with the partnership as or on behalf of a party having an adverse interest; and refraining from competing with the partnership before dissolution.
- **Duty of care** is *"limited to refraining from engaging in grossly negligent or reckless conduct, intentional misconduct, or a knowing violation of law."*

Source: `https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-corp/title-2/chapter-5/article-4/section-16404/`

> **Note the asymmetry that matters for our client.** The partnership **duty of care** is a *gross negligence* standard (Corp. Code § 16404(c)) — materially more forgiving than the **trustee** standard of "reasonable care, skill, and caution" (Prob. Code § 16040). But the partnership **duty of loyalty** expressly includes a **duty to account** and to **hold as trustee** any benefit derived from partnership property or information. A managing partner therefore gets a friendlier negligence standard but not a friendlier accounting standard.

**Cal. Corp. Code § 16405** — **VERIFIED-BY-SEARCH**
- **(a)** *"A partnership may maintain an action against a partner for a breach of the partnership agreement, or for the violation of a duty to the partnership, causing harm to the partnership."*
- **(b)** *"A partner may maintain an action against the partnership or another partner for legal or equitable relief, with or without an accounting as to partnership business."*

Source: `https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-corp/title-2/chapter-5/article-4/section-16405/`

> **§ 16405(b) is the direct partner-vs-partner accounting hook**, and it does **not** require dissolution — relief may be had *"with or without an accounting."* This provision is available **against our client** as managing partner, by any partner, including a trustee-partner.

**Leff v. Gunter (1983) 33 Cal.3d 508** — **PARAPHRASE-ONLY, with one secondary-sourced pin cite**
Reported language: *"Partners are trustees for each other, and in all proceedings connected with the conduct of the partnership every partner is bound to act in the highest good faith to his copartner and may not obtain any advantage over him in the partnership affairs by the slightest misrepresentation, concealment, threat or adverse pressure of any kind."*
Sources: `https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/33/508.html`; `https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-supreme-court/1838610.html`
**A secondary source (`https://www.berliner.com/articles/fiduciary-duties-between-partners-and-joint-venturers-101`) attributes this passage to 33 Cal.3d at 514. That pin cite is reported by a secondary source only and was NOT independently confirmed. Do not use it without checking.**

**Cal. Prob. Code § 1061 — "net income from trade or business"** — **PARAPHRASE-ONLY** (see § A above)
A court-filed account reports a trade or business on a **net** basis as a summary line, supported by a schedule — it does not fold gross partnership receipts and disbursements into the trust's own receipt and disbursement schedules.

> **Synthesis for Issue 8 (this is analysis, not authority):** the statutory materials point toward a **two-tier** structure. At the **trust level**, the trustee must report the partnership interest as an asset (§ 16063(a)(2)), report distributions actually received as receipts (§ 16063(a)(1)), report net trade-or-business results on a schedule (§ 1061), and furnish partnership information "relevant to the beneficiary's interest" on request (§ 16061). At the **partnership level**, the books belong to the partnership, and access is governed by Corp. Code § 16403 with enforcement under § 16405(b). **The trustee cannot use the entity as a shield**, because § 16403 gives the trustee-partner the access needed to satisfy § 16061 and § 16063. **But the beneficiary also cannot simply demand partnership books through a trust accounting petition** — the partnership is a separate legal person with its own records regime. **No case confirming this two-tier synthesis was retrieved. Counsel must verify it before advancing it.**

## Application to Our Facts

*Framing note: everything in this section is offered as considerations for counsel. Nothing here predicts any outcome, and no statement below should be read as a forecast of how the Tulare County Superior Court would rule.*

### 1. Counsel may consider establishing the accounting timeline before anything else

The accounting duty is date-driven, and two of the three governing dates are unresolved.

- **Manuel C. Leal appears to have died 3/25/2015**, based on a partnership capital transfer recorded as *"to Steve/Bypass Trust 3/25/15."* Counsel may consider that this is a *bookkeeping inference*, not a proved date of death, and that a certified death certificate should be obtained before any date-dependent argument is made.
- **Hazel J. Leal's date of death is not established in the records reviewed.** Counsel may consider that this is the single most consequential open fact in the matter. Under § 16062(a) the accounting obligation attaches on a change of trustee; under § 15800 the successor co-trustees' duties to *beneficiaries* (as opposed to the settlor) as to the Hazel Revocable Trust did not arise until the power to revoke ended; under § 16061.7 the 60-day notification clock ran from that date; and the **$327,427.02** figure in our counsel's letter is expressly measured *"through Hazel's date of death."* Counsel may consider that a valuation pegged to an unestablished date is vulnerable on that ground alone.
- **The December 7, 2017 Settlement Agreement and Mutual Release** sits between these events and may operate as a temporal boundary on claims. See § 5.

Counsel may consider building a single dated matrix — trust, capacity, trustee identity, accounting period, and limitations trigger — before drafting any petition.

### 2. Counsel may consider which trust is being accounted for, and by whom

Three instruments are in play with different accounting regimes:

- **Hazel J. Leal Revocable Trust (11/3/2008).** Revocable during Hazel's competent lifetime. Under § 15800 and *Giraldin*, the trustee's duties during that period ran to Hazel, not to remainder beneficiaries. Under § 16064(c) no account to beneficiaries was owed for the revocable period. **After Hazel's death**, § 16062(a) attaches (annually, at termination, on change of trustee), and *Giraldin* may permit reaching pre-death conduct to the extent it harmed the beneficiaries. Counsel may consider that the § 15800 **2022 amendment** does not govern conduct predating its effective date, and that the correct statutory version must be matched to each period.
- **Manuel C. Leal Bypass Trust (uta dtd 2/14/05, as amended).** A bypass trust is ordinarily irrevocable on the first settlor's death. If Manuel died 3/25/2015, counsel may consider that the § 16062(a) annual accounting duty attached to the Bypass Trust from that date forward, running to **each beneficiary to whom income or principal is required or authorized to be currently distributed** — which, in the typical bypass structure, would include Hazel as lifetime income beneficiary.
- **Manuel C. Leal Living Trust.** Role and current status not established in the records reviewed.

**Counsel may consider that the identity of the Bypass Trust's trustee from 3/25/2015 forward is a threshold question that has not been answered — and that the answer may be adverse.** See § 9.

### 3. Counsel may consider § 17200 as the procedural vehicle, and the 60-day predicate as a gating item

Counsel may consider that § 17200(b)(5) (settling accounts and passing on trustee acts) and § 17200(b)(7) (compelling information and accounts) supply the petition framework, and that § 17206 gives the court discretion to *"make any orders and take any other action necessary or proper,"* including appointment of a temporary trustee.

Counsel may consider two practical gates:
- **§ 17200(b)(7) requires a written request and a 60-day lapse**, and as to accounts additionally requires that **no report or account was made within the preceding six months.** Counsel may consider confirming, in writing and on a datable record, that these predicates are satisfied before filing.
- **If the Hazel Trust instrument waives accounting**, § 16064(a) still permits the court to compel an account *"upon a showing that it is reasonably likely that a material breach of the trust has occurred."* Counsel may consider that this is a lower threshold than proving breach, and that an omitted partnership interest — if one exists — is the kind of fact that could be marshalled to meet it.

Counsel may consider that under *Esslinger v. Cummins*, remainder-beneficiary status is not an obstacle to standing to compel an account, though that case must be read before it is cited.

### 4. Counsel may consider the limitations exposure in both directions under § 16460

Under § 16460 the three-year period runs from **receipt of an adequately disclosing account or report**, and otherwise from **discovery or constructive discovery.**

Counsel may consider:
- **If the co-trustees never rendered an account**, the three-year clock is a **discovery** clock, not a receipt clock — which generally favors our side on the accounting and omitted-asset claims.
- **But discovery is constructive as well as actual.** Counsel may consider that our client's position as managing partner who kept the partnership books is precisely the kind of fact a court could treat as bearing on when he "reasonably should have discovered" a discrepancy between partnership capital accounts and trust asset schedules. This is a real risk and should be assessed candidly, not minimized.
- **§ 16063(a)(6)** requires an account to carry the three-year warning. Counsel may consider whether any document the co-trustees characterize as an "account or report" carried that notice, and whether its absence bears on the "adequately discloses" question under § 16460.
- Counsel may consider that **§ 16061.8's 120-day period is a trust-contest bar and does not govern breach-of-trust or accounting claims** — a distinction worth preserving in writing before it is blurred in briefing.
- **Laches has not been researched.** Counsel may consider that a laches defense is foreseeable given the 2015 and 2017 dates and that this memo supplies no authority on it.

### 5. Counsel may consider that the 2017 Settlement Agreement and Mutual Release is a two-edged instrument

Counsel may consider that **§ 16464** governs the effect of a beneficiary's release on trustee liability, and that a release is **not** effective where the beneficiary was under an incapacity, **did not know of the beneficiary's rights and of material facts the trustee knew or should have known**, the release was induced by improper conduct of the trustee, or the bargain was not fair and reasonable.

Counsel may consider the following, in both directions:
- **Offensively:** if the release was signed without disclosure of an omitted partnership interest or of undistributed Bypass Trust income, the "did not know of material facts the trustee knew or should have known" prong of § 16464 may be worth developing. **§ 16004(c)** may also be worth examining — if the 2017 agreement was a transaction between a trustee and a beneficiary by which the trustee obtained an advantage, the statutory presumption affecting the burden of proof may apply.
- **Defensively:** the same analysis is available to the co-trustees against our client, and the release may on its face bar claims arising before 12/7/2017. Counsel may consider that a mutual release cuts symmetrically and that **the scope, defined terms, carve-outs, and effective date of the actual agreement must be read before any of this is asserted.** This memo has not seen the agreement.

### 6. Counsel may consider a § 850 petition for any omitted partnership interest

If a partnership interest (or a capital account balance) belonged to a trust but was never formally assigned or scheduled, counsel may consider that:
- **§ 850(a)(3)(B)** reaches the case *"[w]here the trustee has a claim to real or personal property, title to or possession of which is held by another,"* and **§ 856** authorizes an order directing conveyance *"or granting other appropriate relief."*
- ***Estate of Heggstad*** supplies the substantive theory where a written declaration or trust schedule evidences trust ownership without a recorded transfer. Counsel may consider that this requires **the actual trust schedules**, which should be obtained and examined.
- **Standing is a live question.** The § 850(a)(3) text returned names *the trustee* as petitioner. Counsel may consider confirming whether a beneficiary may petition under (a)(3), or whether the petition must be brought by or in the name of a trustee — a question this memo could not resolve.

On **§ 859 double damages**, counsel may consider proceeding with discipline:
- **Bad faith is a separate, required element.** Under *Levin v. Winston-Levin*, even a successful undue-influence showing does not supply it. Counsel may consider that pleading § 859 without an independent, evidence-backed bad-faith theory invites the *Levin* result — recovery of the property without the multiplier.
- Counsel may consider that a **failure to inventory** an asset, standing alone, is a long distance from *"in bad faith wrongfully taken, concealed, or disposed of."* A record of concealment — e.g., affirmative misstatements about what the trust held — would be doing the work, not the omission itself.
- Counsel may consider that § 859 also permits **reasonable attorney's fees and costs in the court's discretion**, which may be the more attainable component.

### 7. Counsel may consider the surcharge theory as breach-plus-causation, not bad faith

Counsel may consider the following structure:
- **§ 16400** — any duty violation is a breach. Candidate duties: § 16060 (keep informed), § 16062 (account), § 16006 (take and keep control of trust property — the omitted-asset duty), § 16009 (keep property separate and designated), § 16040 (reasonable care, skill, and caution).
- **§ 16440(a)** — three alternative measures, and counsel may consider pleading them in the alternative rather than electing early: (1) loss or depreciation with interest; (2) trustee's profit through the breach, with interest; (3) profit that would have accrued. Under *Uzyel v. Kadisha*, **tracing is not required** for (a)(2) disgorgement, and lost-profit/appreciation damages are available under (a)(3).
- **§ 16441** — interest at the **greater** of the legal judgment rate or interest actually received.
- **§ 16420** — the remedial menu, including redress by payment of money, **removal**, and **reduction or denial of trustee compensation**. Counsel may consider that compensation reduction under § 16420(a)(7) is a remedy that does not depend on proving a dollar loss.
- **§ 16402 and Prob. Code § 16403** — because the Hazel Trust has **successor co-trustees**, counsel may consider whether either co-trustee approved, knowingly acquiesced in, concealed, or negligently enabled a breach by the other (§ 16402), and whether either failed to take reasonable steps to compel a **predecessor** trustee to deliver trust property or redress a breach (Prob. Code § 16403(b)) — a theory that maps directly onto an omitted asset.

Counsel may consider that **bad faith is not an element of surcharge**, but that it is decisive at three other points: it **defeats** § 16461 exculpation, it **forecloses** the § 16440(b)/§ 16441(b) equitable excuse, and it is **required** for § 859. Conversely, counsel may consider that the co-trustees' most likely defensive move is **§ 16440(b)** — "acted reasonably and in good faith" — and that anticipating it is more useful than pleading around it.

Counsel may consider that under **§ 16421** the beneficiary's remedies against the trustee are **exclusively in equity**, with the jury-trial consequences that follow. This should be verified before any trial-setting decision.

### 8. Counsel may consider the partnership-interest accounting as a two-tier problem

Counsel may consider that:
- At the **trust level**, § 16063(a)(2) requires a statement of assets and liabilities, which would include a partnership interest; § 16063(a)(1) captures distributions actually received; and Prob. Code § 1061 reports a trade or business on a **net** basis with a supporting schedule.
- At the **partnership level**, **Corp. Code § 16403** gives partners *and former partners* a right to inspect and copy the books, and obliges each partner and the partnership to furnish information reasonably required for the exercise of a partner's rights, and on demand any other information about the partnership's business and affairs, subject to a reasonableness limit.
- **Corp. Code § 16405(b)** permits a partner to sue another partner for legal or equitable relief **"with or without an accounting as to partnership business"** — no dissolution required.
- **Corp. Code § 16404(b)(1)** imposes on every partner a duty **to account to the partnership** and to **hold as trustee** any property, profit, or benefit derived from the use of partnership property or information.

Counsel may consider that the practical consequence is that a **trustee-partner cannot plead inability to obtain partnership records**, because § 16403 supplies the access. Counsel may equally consider that **this argument is fully available in reverse** against the partner who holds the books.

**Counsel may consider that no case authority for this two-tier synthesis was located, and that the synthesis is therefore argument, not authority.**

### 9. THE DEFENSIVE POSTURE — what these same duties would require OF OUR SIDE

This section is written adversely on purpose. Counsel may consider that the following exposure is structural, not speculative, and that it should be assessed before a petition is filed rather than after it draws a cross-petition.

**(a) Our client kept the books, and the record-keeping cases are neutral as to which side benefits.**
*Purdy* and *McCabe* do not say "resolve doubts in favor of beneficiaries." They say the accounting party must **prove every item by satisfactory evidence**, that **the burden is on that party**, and that **doubts arising from that party's own failure to keep proper records are resolved against that party.** Counsel may consider that as managing partner of Manuel C. Leal & Son Dairy Partnership, our client is the party who kept the books that any accounting will be built from. If those books have gaps — missing vouchers, unsupported capital-account entries, undocumented allocations, informal draws — *McCabe*'s "all presumptions are against them" language is available to the co-trustees against our client with no modification.

**(b) The 3/25/15 capital transfer entry is our own document, and it cuts both ways.**
Counsel may consider that the entry *"to Steve/Bypass Trust 3/25/15"* is a record our client created or controlled. It is useful to us as evidence that a partnership interest moved to the Bypass Trust. It is equally useful to the other side as evidence that **our client knew, as of 3/25/2015, that the Bypass Trust held a partnership interest** — which bears directly on constructive discovery under § 16460 and on any laches defense.

**(c) If our client served as trustee of the Bypass Trust, he owes the § 16062 account for the very income he is claiming.**
Counsel may consider that the **$327,427.02** is characterized in our counsel's letter as *"earned but undistributed income from the Manuel C. Leal Bypass Trust through Hazel's date of death."* Counsel may consider the following chain:
1. If the Bypass Trust became irrevocable on Manuel's death (3/25/2015), § 16062(a) required **annual accounts** from that date to each beneficiary entitled to current income or principal.
2. Hazel would ordinarily be that income beneficiary.
3. **Income "earned but undistributed" is, by its own description, income the trustee did not distribute.**
4. Under § 16000-series duties and § 16440(a), a trustee who failed to distribute required income, and who failed to account for it annually, has the exposure — and the person asserting the claim would ordinarily be Hazel's successor in interest, i.e., **the Hazel Trust, of which the defendant co-trustees are the fiduciaries.**

Counsel may consider that **if our client was the Bypass Trust trustee during that period, the $327,427.02 is not obviously an asset of our claim — it may be a measure of our liability.** Counsel may consider that establishing who served as Bypass Trust trustee from 3/25/2015 forward is urgent and should precede any demand letter that repeats the figure. If our client was **not** the trustee, this concern substantially dissolves, and the figure becomes a claim against whoever was.

**(d) Corp. Code § 16405(b) is a ready-made cross-petition.**
Counsel may consider that any partner — including the Hazel Trust or Bypass Trust as a partner, acting through its trustees — may sue our client for legal or equitable relief **with or without an accounting**, without dissolution. Combined with § 16403's books-and-records access right and § 16404(b)(1)'s duty to account for benefits derived from partnership property or information, counsel may consider that a partnership accounting cross-claim is the most predictable responsive pleading in this matter.

**(e) The partnership duty-of-care standard helps us; the loyalty and accounting duties do not.**
Counsel may consider that Corp. Code § 16404(c) limits a partner's duty of care to **gross negligence, recklessness, intentional misconduct, or knowing violation of law** — materially more protective than Prob. Code § 16040's reasonable-care standard. Counsel may consider that this asymmetry is worth preserving by resisting any characterization of our client's **partnership** conduct as **trustee** conduct. Counsel may equally consider that this protection does **not** extend to the § 16404(b)(1) duty to account, which is a loyalty duty, not a care duty.

**(f) Dual-capacity risk.**
Counsel may consider that if our client acted both as managing partner (owing Corp. Code § 16404 duties to co-partners) and as a fiduciary of a trust holding a partnership interest (owing Probate Code duties to that trust's beneficiaries), the two roles create conflict exposure under Prob. Code § 16004, and any transaction between the partnership and the trust would be examined against that backdrop. Counsel may consider mapping every capacity our client held, by date, before taking any position on the co-trustees' capacities.

**(g) Symmetry of the § 16464 release analysis.**
Counsel may consider that every argument for setting aside the 2017 release as to the co-trustees is available to the co-trustees as to our client, and that a mutual release is by construction bilateral.

### Citation traps flagged for counsel

- **Two different § 16403s**: Probate Code § 16403 (successor-trustee liability) vs. **Corporations Code § 16403** (partnership books and records). Always name the code.
- ***Estate of McCabe*** appears at both **98 Cal.App.2d 503** and **87 Cal.App.2d 430**. Confirm which.
- ***Esslinger v. Cummins*** (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 517 is distinct from ***Smith v. Esslinger*** (1994) 26 Cal.App.4th 579.
- ***Hill v. Superior Court***: multiple California cases share this name, including one at 10 Cal.3d 812. Confirm the 2016 Court of Appeal decision.
