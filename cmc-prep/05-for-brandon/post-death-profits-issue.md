## 3. What the Agreement says happens on Hazel's death

### Article XIII — "WITHDRAWAL OR DEATH OF A PARTNER"

> **B.1.** "Sections B.1-3 of this Article … specifically apply to Hazel. Hazel cannot sell her
> Partnership Interest to anyone who is not a member of the Partnership. **If Hazel dissociates
> from the Partnership, either by sale or other transfer of some or all of Hazel's Partnership
> Interest for consideration, as a result of Hazel's death or desire to sell (the 'Dissociating
> Events'), then the Partnership shall purchase her Partnership Interest** at a price determined
> and payable in the same manner as set forth in Article [XIII].B.2."
>
> **B.2.** "**In the event of the death of Hazel, the Partnership shall continue and will not be
> dissolved. The Partnership shall purchase her Partnership Interest at ninety percent (90%) of
> the appraisal value** of her Partnership Interest including any discounts identified in the
> appraisal (the '**Value**'). At the time of purchase, the Partnership shall pay **ten percent
> (10%) of the Value** on the note to purchase the Partnership Interest. The note will be
> **amortized over a twenty (20) year term, at four percent (4%) interest.** The Partnership note
> will be **due in ten (10) years.**"

### Three features counsel may consider decisive on the contract text

**(a) The Agreement itself calls Hazel's death a "Dissociating Event."**
Art. XIII.B.1 defines the term that way in express words: "If Hazel dissociates … **as a result of
Hazel's death** … (the '**Dissociating Events**')." Art. XIII.J repeats it — "within thirty (30)
days of **Hazel's dissociation or death**." **The parties contracted for the characterisation
defendants now dispute.**

**(b) The buyout is mandatory, not optional.** "the Partnership **shall** purchase her Partnership
Interest." It is not an option Steve may exercise; it is an obligation triggered by the
Dissociating Event.

**(c) The Agreement supplies its own compensation for deferred payment — 4% interest, not
profits.** Art. XIII.B.2 prescribes 10% down and a note "amortized over a twenty (20) year term,
**at four percent (4%) interest**," due in ten years. Settlement Agreement ¶7(b) is identical.
**The parties expressly addressed what the selling side receives while it waits to be paid, and
what they chose was interest at 4%.**

> **Counsel may consider framing it this way:** on defendants' reading, the Trust would receive
> (i) 90% of appraised value, (ii) 4% interest on the deferred balance, **and** (iii) a
> continuing 37.5% profit share — three returns on one interest, for a period during which the
> Agreement says the Partnership "shall purchase" that interest.

### (d) The asymmetry in Article XIII.D is deliberate and worth noting

> **D.** "In the event of the **death of Steve or Marla**, the Partnership shall not be dissolved
> and his or her interest in the Partnership **shall transfer according to the terms of his or her
> Will, Trust, or by intestacy, and shall not be subject to any buy out provision** of this
> Amended Agreement."

**Steve's and Marla's interests pass by estate plan and are expressly NOT bought out. Hazel's is
mandatorily bought out.** The Agreement treats the two sides differently on purpose. Counsel may
consider that this supports reading Art. XIII.B as an exit mechanism — Hazel's side was always
intended to leave the Partnership on her death, while Steve's side continues.

## 4. Article VI.E answers the "excess distributions" question too

> **Article VI.E.** "A credit balance in a Partner's Drawing Account shall be treated as a
> **liability of the Partnership to that Partner**… **A debit balance in a Partner's Drawing
> Account, whether occasioned by drawings in excess of his share of Partnership profits** or by
> charging him for his share of Partnership loss, **shall be treated as a liability of that
> Partner to the Partnership and shall not reduce his interest in the capital of the
> Partnership.**"

> ### ⚠️ THIS VINDICATES THE SUBSTANCE OF OUR OFFSET AND CONFIRMS THE LABEL IS WRONG
>
> Our March 3, 2025 letter (Ex. R) deducts **$262,592.00** of "**excess distributions above her
> partnership share during Hazel's lifetime**" plus **$194,352.65** of post-death distributions.
> The Complaint (¶¶46, 64, 107) calls this a "**negative capital account**," and our own reviewed
> financials show a positive capital account — which this workspace flagged as a contradiction
> (`adverse_analysis.md` A-4).
>
> **Article VI.E explains why both are true and which label is right.** Excess drawings create a
> **debit balance in the Drawing Account**, which the Agreement makes "**a liability of that
> Partner to the Partnership**" and which "**shall not reduce his interest in the capital of the
> Partnership.**"
>
> **So the capital account is *supposed* to stay positive while a drawing-account debit
> accumulates. They are separate ledgers by contract.** Our offset is not a negative capital
> account — it is a **drawing-account debit balance owed by the partner to the partnership**, and
> the Agreement says so in terms.
>
> **Counsel should consider amending the Complaint's characterisation to track Article VI.E.** As
> pleaded it is contradicted by our own financials; as Article VI.E describes it, it is supported
> by the Agreement.
>
> **Still needed:** Frazer's capital-account summary — Exhibit B to the March 3, 2025 letter
> (`open_items.md` OI-5, OI-28).

## 5. The counterarguments — stated at full strength

This section is written against our own position. Counsel should not go to the CMC without it.

### 5.1 — "The partner is a TRUST, and a trust is not dissociated by a trustee's death"

**This is their best argument.** The partner named in Article I.B is "**THE HAZEL J. LEAL
REVOCABLE TRUST**" — an entity, not Hazel personally. Hazel's death substituted Ashley and Susan
as successor co-trustees; the Trust itself did not die.

**Corporations Code § 16601 — UNVERIFIED, counsel must confirm.** Research memo R4 reports that
§ 16601(7) treats a trust-partner as dissociated on "distribution of the trust's entire
transferable interest in the partnership, **but not merely by reason of the substitution of a
successor trustee**." If that is the statutory text, **the default rule is that this Trust remains
a partner.**

**Our answers, in order of strength:**

1. **The Agreement's defined term reaches the Trust.** "Hazel" is defined to mean Hazel "**Trustee
   of THE HAZEL J. LEAL REVOCABLE TRUST**." Art. XIII.B.1 makes "**Hazel's death**" the
   Dissociating Event **for that defined party** — i.e. for the Trust's interest. The parties
   contracted around the default.
2. **Partnership agreements generally govern relations among partners** — counsel should confirm
   the scope and the non-waivable list (**Corp. Code § 16103 — UNVERIFIED**).
3. **The Agreement was drafted with this exact structure in view.** Every partner is a trust or a
   trustee. The drafters knew Hazel would hold through a revocable trust and still keyed the
   buyout to "Hazel's death."
4. **Art. XIII.D confirms the parties knew how to say "passes by trust and is not bought out"** —
   they said it for Steve and Marla and did not say it for Hazel.

**Honest assessment: argument 1 is strong on the text, but it is a contract-versus-statute
question and counsel must verify § 16601 and § 16103 before relying on it.** This is the single
most important item to run in Westlaw.

### 5.2 — "The buyout never closed, so nothing converted"

Art. XIII.B.2 says "**At the time of purchase**, the Partnership shall pay ten percent (10%) of
the Value." **There has been no purchase, no 10% payment, and no note.** Defendants will argue the
Trust cannot be treated as a paid-out seller when it has received nothing, and that a party
carrying the risk of an unliquidated interest for four years should share in the returns.

**Our answers:**
1. **Interest is the contractual answer to delay** (Art. XIII.B.2; Settlement ¶7(b)) — and it runs
   at 4%, which is what our own July 2026 offer tendered.
2. **The valuation date is fixed at death** by the Settlement Agreement, TIC ¶11 and Art. XIII.J
   ("as of the date of Hazel's death"). If value is fixed at death, counsel may argue the economic
   position was fixed at death too.
3. **Corp. Code § 16701 — UNVERIFIED.** R4 and R3 both report that RUPA's buyout remedy is value
   as of the date of dissociation **plus interest from that date**. If so, the statutory model
   matches the contract: interest, not profits.

**Honest assessment: the equitable pull of "they haven't been paid anything in four years" is
real, and a court may feel it.** Counsel should be ready with the delay chronology (§5.3) and
with the fact that **we tendered interest**.

### 5.3 — "Steve caused the delay, so he cannot benefit from it"

Defendants' brief: "Steve's unlawful conduct, secrecy, and breaches… are the cause of the present
dispute." Our position is the reverse — they revoked the agreed appraiser and withheld the reports.

**This is a genuinely contested factual question** and it is the one the post-death profits issue
may actually turn on. **Our documentary record here is now strong** (see
`email-exhibits-extract.md`):
- Esraelian **proposed** Correia-Xavier (Ex. E) and **engaged him for the buyout** (Ex. G)
- Xavier was **waiting on the parties to define buyout scope** (Stan Xavier's 5/8/2023 email, quoted in Ex. K)
- Esraelian promised the appraisal "**by next week**" on 8/18/2023 (Ex. I) and had not produced it on 9/26/2023 (Ex. J)
- Defendants unilaterally retained J. Hower and then Moss Adams (Exs. K, N)
- Ashley routed reports to Jace with a stated preference they not go to counsel (Ex. P)

**Against us:** the $850,894.54 demand of 9/26/2023 went unanswered in this record; no monthly
accountings under Settlement ¶8 have been located; and defendants say they were waiting on
information from the Dairy's CPA.

1. **Practical construction.** The party now arguing that Hazel's side ceased to be a partner on

   defendants' characterisation; it is our own reviewed financial statement.

**Our best documentary responses — and counsel should weigh them honestly:**

1. **Bookkeeping convention is not legal entitlement.** Where a mandatory buyout is triggered but

   purchase settles. Frazer prepared these statements; **counsel should ask Frazer why the

2. **Allocation is not distribution.** Art. VII.C provides that "**All Profits and Losses

   does not, the claim is overstated on our own books.

**Honest assessment: response 3 is strong and quantified; responses 1 and 2 are real but they are
explanations of our own conduct, which is a weaker posture than pointing at theirs.** Counsel
should decide early whether to (a) argue the accounting presentation is a convention that does
not control the legal question, or (b) accept the allocation framework and litigate the *amount*
— pressing the FY2023 loss, the offsets, and the valuation date. **These are different cases and
the choice should be deliberate.**

### 5.5 — "Article VII allocates profits to Partners, full stop"

> **Art. VII.A/B.** "Partnership Profits, Losses, gains and credits **shall be allocated among the
> Partners in accordance with their respective Partnership Interest**… The balance to be

Defendants will read this as unqualified. **Our answer is that it turns entirely on whether the
Trust is still a "Partner" after the Dissociating Event** — which is §5.1. **Article VII does not

Art. VII in either direction.

## 9. Two drafting defects in the Agreement, noted for completeness

- **Article cross-references are wrong throughout Article XIII.** The text repeatedly says "this
  Article XII" and "Article XII.B.2" where it plainly means **XIII**. Art. XIII.B.1 refers to
  "Article XII.B.2" for the price mechanism; the mechanism is at **XIII.B.2**. Counsel should be
  ready to address this if defendants argue ambiguity — and note that **the Complaint's ¶105
  citation to "Article XIII, Section B.2" is CORRECT**, as is ¶22's citation to §J. They govern
  different things (the buyout right and the appraiser mechanism respectively).
  **This corrects the flag raised at `complaint-extract.md` §6, item 4.**
- **Article XIII.I cites "Sections 15672-15673 of the California Corporations Code"** for an
  assignee's rights. Those are **limited partnership** provisions, and this is a **general**
  partnership formed "pursuant to the Uniform Partnership Act." Counsel should confirm the correct
  current sections before quoting the clause.
- **Preamble dates the Original Agreement March 16, 1983**; the Settlement Agreement Recitals A
  and C say **March 10, 1983**. A six-day discrepancy in our own instruments. → **OI-16**.
