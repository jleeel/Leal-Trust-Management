## 1. WHAT "WINNING" ACTUALLY LOOKS LIKE ON THIS RECORD

**Both sides want the buyout to close.** Defendants' own mediation brief treats the buyout as
inevitable — their fight is price, profits, and leverage. The realistic win is therefore:
**close the buyout at a defensible number, without letting the fiduciary/accounting counterattack
metastasize into its own case.** A trial "victory" ordering defendants to employ two specific
appraisers is the weakest version of winning — the relief as pleaded has a possible Civ. Code
§ 3390 problem (unverified), Moss Adams is now Baker Tilly, and the § 998 vehicle built on the
same structure is defect-laden.

### The gap, decomposed (rough demonstrative arithmetic — not opinions of value)

Our July 2026 offer: **$5,705,257.43**. Their June 2025 demand: **$7,359,766** + 2025 profit +
lease credits. The ~$1.65M+ gap is four levers, each worth roughly half a million to a million
dollars:

| Lever | Our position | Theirs | Rough swing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Real property basis | Correia-Xavier ($14.05M adjusted) | Average of both appraisals ($16.0M net) | ~$0.5–0.7M |
| 2. Minority discount | 30% | 15% | ~$0.8–0.9M |
| 3. Post-death compensation | 4% interest ($677,830) | 37.5% profit share ($1,617,761 + 2025 + leases) | ~$0.9M+ |
| 4. Offsets | −$456,944.65 excess distributions | $0 | ~$0.5M |

**Strategic observation for counsel:** the appraiser-identity fight (lever 1) is the *smallest*
lever, yet it is what the Complaint is built around. Levers 2 and 3 are where the money is, and
on both of those **the contract text is our strongest asset** — the TIC/Settlement formula
contemplates identified discounts (the prior Xavier work used 20–30%), and Art. XIII.B expressly
prices the post-death wait at 4% interest.

### The three pillars of the affirmative case

1. **The contract already decides the big questions.** Hazel's death is a defined "Dissociating
   Event" (Art. XIII.B.1); the buyout is mandatory ("shall purchase"); delay compensation is 4%
   interest, not profits (Art. XIII.B.2, Settlement ¶7(b)); Steve/Marla's interests pass by
   estate plan while Hazel's is bought out (Art. XIII.D) — a deliberate asymmetry.
2. **The joint-appointment record is theirs.** Esraelian proposed Xavier (Ex. E), engaged him
   with the TIC Agreement attached "for purposes of the… Buy out provisions" (Ex. G), Xavier
   was waiting on joint scope (his 5/8/2023 email, quoted in Ex. K), and defendants revoked
   before scope was set. Every element is in writing, mostly in their counsel's words.
3. **Their own neutral undercut their "errors" story.** Moss Adams found "no obvious errors or
   omissions" and gave Xavier's report a "slight advantage" (Ex. Q) — while also declining to
   pick a number and recommending a jointly hired third appraiser.

### The decision counsel should make first (framed, not made, here)

**Whether to keep fighting for "Xavier's number binds" or to pivot to "complete the valuation
now" via the contract's own three-appraiser fallback / a court-appointed appraiser.**

- *Hold the Xavier line:* maximizes lever 1, keeps the breach narrative clean — but our own
  pleading concedes the appraisal omitted the contractual discounts, Exhibit Q declined to
  endorse the number, the fallback clocks expired unused on both sides, and the pleaded relief
  has the § 3390 problem.
- *Pivot to completion:* accept a jointly appointed third appraiser (or court appointment),
  which instantly moots their "errors" narrative, aligns us with Moss Adams' own
  recommendation and the contract's fallback — and concentrates the fight on levers 2 and 3,
  where our text arguments are strongest. Cost: gives up part of lever 1.

**Either way, the case theory should stop being "make them use our appraiser" and become
"enforce the buyout the contract mandates, priced the way the contract says."**

### Where we can lose

Honestly: **not on the buyout — on the counterattack.** Their theory is covenant breach and
information suppression by the managing partner (A-13), and our exposed flanks are real:
- ~58 months of ¶8 monthly accountings with no evidence of compliance
- the unanswered 9/26/2023 demand asserting an "agreed" $850,894.54
- the Bypass Trust dissolved and its 7.5% transferred to our client on 1/1/2023, with no
  notice shown (A-14 / OI-34)
- our own books carrying an "Estate of Hazel Leal" profit column post-death
- the LOC/capital-expenditure covenants we cannot yet prove we honored
- pre-2017 record gaps, restatements, and a qualified accountants' conclusion

**They are running a forensic review. We have served no discovery. Closing that asymmetry is as
important as any argument.**
