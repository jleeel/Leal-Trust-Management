## 3. Both ways

**For us:**
1. A witness who was **in Hazel's circle in 2020–22**, who says Hazel sought her own
   banker/stockbroker information and said Susan was "messing with" her accounts, and that
   facility staff were instructed to call Danielle. That bears on who controlled Hazel's finances
   and access in her last years. The Morgan Stanley and account questions (A-54, OI-59, OI-62)
   sit nearby.
2. **Corroborates the care-cost record**, and that Hazel's draws in 2020–22 were overwhelmingly
   her care. That is useful for Demand 6 and the $262,592 offset story.
3. Mary presents as **mentally sharp** in this excerpt: dates, ages, finances, her own
   bookkeeping. If her testimony matters, her age argues for counsel acting soon.

**Against us, or limits:**
1. **Relevance to the buyout case is indirect.** Nothing in the excerpt touches the partnership,
   the buyout, valuation, post-death profits or Hazel's intentions about the dairy. A fight over
   Susan's handling of Hazel's money risks looking like a trust contest. Hazel's trust has a
   no-contest clause, and Steve was disinherited by the 11/10/2017 restatement (A-79). How that
   interacts is counsel's call (UNVERIFIED).
2. **Mary is not neutral.** She was in a police-involved confrontation with Danielle. She has
   received attorney letters accusing her. She calls the trust attorney a "nasty witch" and says
   Susan should be put out if Mary dies. Cross-examination would center on bias. Her age invites
   memory questions despite the competence indicators.
3. **The tape captures Steve feeding a theory to the witness** ("I guess that was the intention…
   get to the bottom of something they didn't want to"). If produced, it supports a
   "plaintiff shaped her account" argument.
4. **Hearsay.** Most of what matters is Hazel's words relayed by Mary.
5. **Consent to record is not shown** (A-85). If Mary did not consent, the recording itself is a
   problem, separate from her live testimony (Penal Code § 632, UNVERIFIED).
