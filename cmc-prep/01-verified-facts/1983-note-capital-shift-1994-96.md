## 2. The arithmetic (calc)

From 12/31/1993 to 1/1/1997, which covers 1994, 1995 and 1996:

| Partner | Change in capital |
|---|---|
| Manuel | **+$270,501** ($180,568 → $451,069) |
| Steve | **−$126,090** ($192,459 → $66,369) |
| Total | +$144,411 |

**If profits were split 50/50** (as printed in 1997 and inferred in 1993) and nothing else
happened, the swing would require Steve to have withdrawn **$396,591 more than Manuel** over the
three years. For comparison, Steve's withdrawals were about $40K in 1993 and $1.5K in 1997, and
Manuel's were the larger in both years. That is implausible. **A reallocation of capital from
Steve to Manuel is the natural reading.**

The implied transfer T is (396,591 + Manuel's withdrawals − Steve's withdrawals) ÷ 2:

| Assumption about 1994–96 withdrawals | Implied transfer |
|---|---|
| Equal withdrawals | ≈ **$198,296** |
| 1997 pattern (Manuel +$14,748/yr) | ≈ **$220,418** |
| 1993 pattern (Manuel +$42,797/yr) | ≈ **$262,491** |

**The note's principal, $212,035, falls inside that range.** Unpaid interest at 9% simple is
$19,083 a year, about $286K over 1982–1996 if none was ever paid.

**A competing explanation that also fits.** The 1983 Partnership Agreement set initial capital
at 75/25 (Manuel $636,105 / Steve $212,035). If the accountants allocated 1994–96 profits 75/25,
or made a catch-up reallocation to the agreement's percentages, much of the swing is explained
*without* any note payoff. For example, with roughly $600K of three-year income the implied
transfer drops to about $60K–$137K. The 1994–96 profit split is unknown.

## 3. Grade and significance (both ways)

**Grade: PARTIAL / INDETERMINATE.** The capital shift is SUPPORTED by printed figures. That it
was a payoff of the note is NOT ESTABLISHED. It is consistent with the numbers but not
documented.

**If it is proven, it helps:**
- **The note allowed prepayment without consent**, so a voluntary 1990s payoff needed no one's
  sign-off.
- **The value went to the payees' side.** A transfer credited to Manuel's capital moved value to
  the community of Manuel and Hazel, the note's payees. That capital later passed to Hazel's
  trust and the Bypass column. So any renewed claim on the note's full balance (Thompson's
  2017 P&I figure was $879,945.25) would be a double recovery.

**What cuts against it:**
- There are no 1994–96 records. The 75/25 reallocation explanation is live.
- The note requires payment "in lawful money". Whether a book transfer counts as payment is a
  question for counsel (UNVERIFIED).
- **The same theory was raised in March 2017, before the 12/7/2017 Settlement Agreement.** ¶6 then
  compromised the note at $350,000. The settlement likely governs whatever happened in the 1990s
  (accord, novation and release effects are UNVERIFIED; counsel decides).
- **The live exposure is still A-56: no proof the $350,000 was ever paid.** This hypothesis does
  not answer that.
