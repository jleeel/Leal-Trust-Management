## 2. Most journal entries are year-end closings, not reclasses

Frazer closes each account to capital on January 1. Each closing equals the prior year's activity.
In 505000, for example, the 1/1/2017 entry is +$14,691.87, which equals the 2016 checks.

**Neither account was closed before 2013.** 505000 reached −$167,347.70 by 12/31/2012. Then the
1/1/2013 "To tie out capital" and 12/31/2013 "**To tie to FRAZER @ 12.31.12**" entries plug both
accounts to Frazer's balances:

**Consequence:** for 2009–2012, the coding in QuickBooks does **not** show how an item was finally
treated in the financial statements.

## 6. United of Omaha (Demand 13): the books treat it as Steve's personal

**A Frazer entry dated 12/31/2016 moves $4,126.20 of United of Omaha "life insurance" into
Steve's personal draws.** The other side of the entry is 514000 Medical–Steve. That equals two
semiannual premiums. The register codes:
- the 2016 premiums to 760000 Insurance–General;
- the 2018 premiums directly to 514000 Medical–Steve.

So which checks the entry covers is unclear. The audit trail would show when it was entered.

**Either way, the books themselves treat this policy as Steve's personal, in 2016 and 2018.**
The other premiums remain coded as a general insurance expense with no reclass shown:
- 2013, 2015, 2017, 2019, 2020, 2021 (×2) and 2022: **8 × $2,063.10 = $16,504.80**;
- 2012: indeterminate.

The three checks in Demand 13 (2019, 2020, 2022) are among them. **This makes Demand 13 harder,
not easier.** Who is insured and who is beneficiary still needs the policy, but the books point
to Steve.
