## 1. CLAIM: The $45,064 draw (2000) and the Kaweah Drive purchase

### Grade: **CONTRADICTED (as to the premise) / UNSUPPORTED (as to the comparison)**

**What the record actually shows.**

$45,064 is located. It is **not a draw on a date.** It is an **annual category total**
for a full fiscal year:

> MANUEL LEAL & SON DAIRY, Statement of Partners' Capital Accounts for the Year Ended
> December 31, 2000 (See Accountants' Review Report), Exhibit C —
> Deduct withdrawals: Donations $4,662 (Manuel $4,191 / Steve $471); Medical $2,435
> (Manuel $1,953 / Steve $482); **Personal $48,429 (Manuel $45,064 / Steve $3,365)**;
> Total withdrawals $55,526 (Manuel $51,208 / Steve $4,318).
> — Equity Accounts 1997-2015.pdf (S-1 · Drive `1O7OGL798JcaTXginVNMIUgKtNV-ckK0l`),
> FY2000 statement.

So: **$45,064 is Manuel C. Leal's aggregate "Personal" withdrawal line for the twelve
months ended December 31, 2000.** It is a column subtotal in an annual reviewed
financial statement.

**Consequences — stated plainly:**

1. **There is no draw date to extract.** The figure is not a transaction. It has no
   date beyond the fiscal year it summarizes. Any single date attributed to it would be
   invented.
2. **The gap in days cannot be computed and is not reported here.** Computing a
   day-count gap requires (a) a dated draw and (b) a dated purchase/recording. The
   record supplies neither. Reporting a number would be fabrication.
3. **No Kaweah Drive purchase or recording document exists in the record.** Searched by
   title and full text; nothing responsive. (Full-text "Kaweah" hits resolve to the
   Kaweah groundwater subbasin — an unrelated agricultural-water matter in the same
   Drive.) **No source.**
4. Consistent with the instruction, **no characterization of "alignment" is offered**,
   and none could be supported on this record even if a date existed.

**What would be required to answer the question as originally framed:**
the FY2000 general ledger, check register, or bank statements for the partnership —
i.e. transaction-level data. See `open_items.md`, items OI-2 and OI-6.

**Adverse note for counsel:** the $45,064 sits inside a line captioned "Personal." The
reviewed statements categorize withdrawals as Donations / Medical / Income taxes /
Personal / Itemized deductions. The statements themselves supply **no narrative** for
what any "Personal" withdrawal purchased. Attributing this sum to a specific real-estate
purchase is an inference the reviewed statements do not support in either direction.

reviewed financial statements, it is unambiguous, and it is the single strongest
documentary fact against a disparity narrative. It is addressed in
`/04-adverse-analysis/adverse_analysis.md`, item A-1.

### 4.3 Additional adverse feature of the same series

Across FY1997–FY2016 the aggregate favors Steve's position on totals, but the
**trend reverses in the later years**: from FY2010 onward the two columns converge, and
in the two years where they cross, they cross against us. A disparity argument built on
the full 20-year aggregate is materially weaker if the court's attention is drawn to the
FY2010–FY2014 window.
