## 5. The law, verified vs. open (details: `02-legal-research/verification-report-courtlistener.md`)

**Verified against opinion text this month (CourtListener; KeyCite still required):**
- *Han v. Hallberg* (2019) 35 Cal.App.5th 621 — trustee substitution ≠ dissociation
  (§ 16601(8)); **review granted then DISMISSED 8/31/2020 — the opinion stands.** Their best
  case. Our distinction: their buyout keyed to "death of a partner"; **ours keys to "Hazel's
  death" by name and labels it a Dissociating Event.** Note the published split with *Presta*
  (2009) 179 Cal.App.4th 909; Tulare (5th Dist.) is bound by neither.
- *Rappaport v. Gelfand* (2011) — § 16701(b) verbatim: buyout price carries **interest from the
  dissociation date** — matches the contract's 4% answer.
- *Poster* (1990) 52 Cal.3d 266 — § 998 + § 1013 = 35 days (our July offer is expired).
- *Gorobets* (Cal. 8/6/2026) + *Mostafavi* fn. 6 — § 998 terms/alternatives must be **in the
  offer itself**, offeror's burden. Both cut against the old offer's cover-letter structure.
- *Bewick* (1945) 26 Cal.2d 92 (Traynor) — when a party's refusal breaks the agreed appraisal
  mechanism, **the court fixes the price and decrees specific performance**; *Petrolink* (2018) —
  court-appointed Evid. Code § 730 appraiser. **This is the cure for the Complaint's § 3390
  problem** (*Woolley*: no specific performance of personal services — the current prayer asks
  the court to order defendants to "employ" two named non-parties).

**The dispositive open question:** whether the agreement's express "Dissociating Events"
definition contracts around § 16601(8) — i.e., **§ 16103(b)'s non-waivable list. One Westlaw
session: KeyCite Han, pull §§ 16103/16601/16603/16701 and § 16404's limits on Art. III.**
