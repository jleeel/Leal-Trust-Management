## A-13. The $100,000 borrowing covenant and the monthly accounting duty — defendants' actual theory

**Severity: HIGHEST alongside A-1. This is the case they are actually building.**

Their brief does not lead with draw disparity. It leads with **covenant breach and
information suppression**, and the covenants are in the contract:

> **¶11(a)** "…Steve may borrow from an already established credit line without approval of
> the Partners **up to $100,000.00 in a thirty (30) day period.**"
> **¶11** "…any capital expenses of **$100,000 or more, will be approved by both partners**…
> **no loans shall be made to any partner without approval of all partners; no borrowing…
> without approval of all partners.**"
> **¶8** "…**an accounting of the Partnership operations will be provided to Hazel on the 15th
> of each month**… the Partnership shall respond with answers to any questions raised by Hazel
> on or before the 25th of each month."

**Their allegations, in their words:**
- "records confirm Steve has been in **breach** of the Settlement Agreement which prohibits him from drawing more than $100,000 from the Dairy's line of credit in a 30 day period. **Not only has Steve routinely breached this covenant**…"
- "…whether Steve has used this line of credit to **funnel profits to himself under the guise of expenses** so that he could withhold profit distributions to Hazel/the Trust."
- "…whether Steve inappropriately **reinvested profits… into capital expenditures exceeding $100,000**… **thereby increasing the value of the company for his benefit.**"
- "a **forensic review of the Dairy's books** reveal[s] irregularities in the expenses and profit distributions."
- Footnote 1: "Steve has breached the Settlement Agreement on **multiple occasions**."

**Why this is worse for us than the draw analysis.** These are **bright-line contractual
covenants with numeric thresholds**, not fuzzy fiduciary standards. Compliance is provable or
disprovable from the line-of-credit statements and the capital-expenditure records. And ¶8 is
a **monthly** obligation — roughly **58 months** between December 2017 and Hazel's death on
September 23, 2022. Every missed month is a discrete alleged breach.

**Our best documentary response:**

1. **¶11(a) permits borrowing up to $100,000 per 30-day period without approval**, and
   expressly deems it "part of the day-to-day operations." Ordinary operating draws on the
   line are contractually authorized. The question is only whether any 30-day window exceeded
   the cap — a countable question.
2. **¶11(a) also constrains Hazel:** approval of borrowing over $100,000 "**is not to be
   unreasonably withheld and an approval decision must be made within ten (10) days.**" If
   requests were made and not answered within ten days, that is a defense, and potentially a
   counter-breach.
3. **TIC ¶1(b)** obliged the Partnership to pay "any [and] all expenses related to the
