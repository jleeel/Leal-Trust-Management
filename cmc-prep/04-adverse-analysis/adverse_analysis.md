## A-1. FY2014: Steve drew $57,422 more than Manuel/Hazel — and it is in our own reviewed financials

**Severity: HIGHEST. This is the single strongest documentary fact against us.**

**What Cunningham has.** Our own reviewed financial statements:

> Year ended December 31, 2014 — Total withdrawals:
> **Manuel Leal $110,081 · Steve Leal $167,503**
> Of which "Personal": **Manuel $7,333 · Steve $120,155**
> — Equity Accounts 1997-2015.pdf (Drive `1O7OGL798JcaTXginVNMIUgKtNV-ckK0l`), FY2015/2014 statement

**How she uses it.** Any argument that Hazel's side over-drew from the partnership is met
with a single page from our own accountant's statements showing that in FY2014 Steve took
**16 times** as much in the "Personal" category as Manuel did. She does not need an expert.
She needs one exhibit.

**She also has FY2011:** Steve $42,119 vs. Manuel $41,507. Smaller ($612), but it makes
FY2014 look like a pattern rather than an outlier, and it lets her say "in the two most
recent years before Manuel's death, Steve out-drew his father."

**Our best documentary response:**

1. **Composition, not amount.** Manuel's FY2014 draw was dominated by **Medical: $59,962**
   (against Steve's $3,015) — 54% of Manuel's total. Medical withdrawals in a partner's
   final years are not discretionary consumption. On a **discretionary** basis the gap
   narrows considerably. *This is a real point and it is documented in the same statement.*
2. **Income taxes are pass-through, not benefit.** Of Steve's $167,503, **$41,333 was
   income taxes** — tax on partnership income allocated to him, not personal enrichment.
   Netting taxes from both sides: Steve $126,170 vs. Manuel $72,972. The gap narrows from
   $57,422 to $53,198 — *this response is weaker than it first appears and counsel should
   not lean on it.*
3. **The 20-year aggregate still runs the other way**, substantially. See the full series
   in `verified_facts_memo.md` §4.1.

**Honest assessment: response (1) is genuinely good. Response (2) is thin. Response (3)
invites the reply that we are averaging away the years that matter.** Counsel should
expect to concede FY2014 and fight on composition rather than deny the number.

## A-3. The § 998 offer contains no monetary term

**Severity: HIGH — structural, and largely unaddressed in our file.**

**What Cunningham has.** The § 998 offer asks only that defendants employ Correia-Xavier
and Moss Adams to appraise. **No dollar figure appears anywhere in it.** The
$5,705,257.43 lives only in the cover letter.

**How she uses it.** § 998(c)(1) turns on whether the party who rejected the offer failed
to obtain a **"more favorable judgment."** Cunningham argues that an offer with no
monetary term cannot be compared to a judgment, so cost-shifting cannot attach as a matter
of law — and that placing the money in a *cover letter* rather than the offer was a choice
we made. She may add that the offer is too uncertain to be capable of valuation.

**She has a further point that is genuinely awkward:** our § 998 offer directs defendants
to employ **Moss Adams** to appraise the partnership — but on **our own account**, Moss
Adams *already completed* that valuation on **November 18, 2024** ($2,840,000)
(Mediation Brief p.7). The offer asks for work our own brief says is done.

**Our best documentary response:**

1. The offer tracks the **specific performance relief pleaded in the Complaint** — which
   is the point of a § 998 offer in a specific-performance action. An offer that mirrors
   the prayer is not defective merely because the prayer is non-monetary.
2. The Settlement Agreement, TIC Agreement, and Partnership Agreement supply the **price
   formula** (90% of appraised value with identified discounts). The offer is therefore
   arguably certain-by-reference even without a stated sum.
3. Legal authority on non-monetary § 998 offers is in **R1**.

**Honest assessment: response (2) is the real argument and it depends on instruments we
have not reviewed.** The Settlement Agreement, TIC Agreement, and Partnership Agreement
are **not in this record** — only our brief's characterization of them. Until counsel
reads the actual buyout clauses, we do not know whether the formula is self-executing
enough to make the offer certain. **This is a documentary gap, not a legal one.** See OI-4.

**On the Moss Adams point we have no good documentary response in this record.** Counsel
should clarify the intended scope of ¶2 of the offer before the CMC.
